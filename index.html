Golden State Warriors

A professional basketball team based in San Francisco, founded in Philadelphia in 1946, and winner of seven league championships.

History

Each era below starts with a short summary. Open an era to read more.

  1. 1946–1962

    Philadelphia beginnings

    A founding member of the BAA, the Philadelphia Warriors won the league's first championship in 1947 behind scoring star Joe Fulks. A second title came in 1956 with Hall of Famers Paul Arizin, Tom Gola, and Neil Johnston.

    More about this era

    In 1959 the Warriors added Wilt Chamberlain, who won Rookie of the Year and Most Valuable Player in the same season. On March 2, 1962, in Hershey, Pennsylvania, he scored 100 points against the New York Knicks. It is still the NBA's single-game scoring record.

  2. 1962–1971

    San Francisco Warriors

    The team moved west in 1962 and took the name San Francisco Warriors. Chamberlain led them to the 1964 NBA Finals, where they lost to the Boston Celtics.

    More about this era

    Chamberlain was traded to the Philadelphia 76ers in January 1965, and the team finished the season 17–63, the league's worst record. The rebuild was quick: rookie Rick Barry and center Nate Thurmond took the Warriors back to the Finals in 1967.

  3. 1971–1985

    Golden State and the 1975 upset

    After moving most home games to Oakland, the team became the Golden State Warriors in 1971. In 1975, coach Al Attles's team swept the heavily favored Washington Bullets for the franchise's third title, with Rick Barry named Finals MVP.

    More about this era

    Forward Jamaal Wilkes was Rookie of the Year on that 1975 team. The success didn't last: the Warriors missed the playoffs in most seasons from the late 1970s to the mid-1980s.

  4. 1985–1997

    The “Run TMC” years

    Under coach Don Nelson, guards Tim Hardaway and Mitch Richmond and forward Chris Mullin, known as “Run TMC” from their first initials, played a fast, high-scoring style that brought the Warriors back to the playoffs.

    More about this era

    The trio played only two seasons together before Richmond was traded to the Sacramento Kings in 1991. Chris Webber was Rookie of the Year in 1994 but was traded early the next season after a falling-out with Nelson.

  5. 1997–2009

    A long drought, then “We Believe”

    The Warriors missed the playoffs for 12 straight seasons. In 2007, the “We Believe” team led by Baron Davis beat the 67-win, top-seeded Dallas Mavericks. It was the first time an eighth seed had beaten a first seed in a best-of-seven series.

    More about this era

    Don Nelson returned as head coach in 2006 to lead that run. The next season the team won 48 games but still missed the playoffs in a crowded Western Conference.

  6. 2009–2019

    The Stephen Curry era and a dynasty

    Stephen Curry (drafted in 2009), Klay Thompson (2011), and Draymond Green (2012) became the core of a team bought by Joe Lacob and Peter Guber in 2010. Under Steve Kerr, hired in 2014, the Warriors reached five straight NBA Finals and won the title in 2015, 2017, and 2018.

    More about this era

    The 2015–16 team went 73–9, the best regular-season record in NBA history, but lost the Finals to Cleveland after leading the series 3–1. Kevin Durant signed that summer and was Finals MVP in both 2017 and 2018. The run ended in the 2019 Finals, a loss to the Toronto Raptors in which Durant and Thompson were both injured.

  7. 2019–present

    Chase Center and a fourth modern title

    The Warriors moved back to San Francisco in 2019 and opened Chase Center in the Mission Bay neighborhood. After two seasons disrupted by injuries, they won the 2022 championship over the Boston Celtics, and Curry was named Finals MVP for the first time.

    More about this era

    With Curry and Thompson injured for most of 2019–20, the team finished with the league's worst record. Thompson left for the Dallas Mavericks in 2024. In February 2025 the Warriors traded for Jimmy Butler and went on to beat the Houston Rockets in a seven-game first-round series before losing to the Minnesota Timberwolves.

Rivalries

Cleveland Cavaliers

The teams met in four straight NBA Finals from 2015 to 2018, a first for any pair of NBA teams. The Warriors won three of them.

Los Angeles Lakers

Both franchises moved to California in the early 1960s. The rivalry heated up after LeBron James joined the Lakers, who beat the Warriors in the 2023 Western Conference semifinals.

Sacramento Kings

A Northern California rivalry between cities about 86 miles apart. The teams first met in the playoffs in 2023, when the Warriors won a hard-fought series in seven games.

Home arenas

  1. 1946–1962 Philadelphia Arena, plus Philadelphia Convention Hall from 1952
  2. 1962–1971 Cow Palace, San Francisco Civic Auditorium, and War Memorial Gymnasium
  3. 1971–2019 Oakland Arena (later called Oracle Arena). The team played the 1996–97 season at San Jose Arena during renovations.
  4. 2019–now Chase Center, San Francisco
